03

Calculating Loss

We cannot simply add the raw errors together. If we do, House 2 (+1000) and House 3 (-250) will partially cancel each other out. The machine would think it's doing a great job even if it's completely wrong.

Instead, we square the error for each house. This turns negative errors positive and aggressively punishes large mistakes.

House 1
(Error)²
(500)² = 250,000
House 2
(Error)²
(1000)² = 1,000,000
House 3
(Error)²
(-250)² = 62,500

04

The Calculus Engine (Finding the Slope)

1. What is a "Partial" Derivative?

Gut Intuition: Imagine you are standing on the side of a 3D mountain. Your GPS coordinates are longitude (w₁) and latitude (w₂). You are blindfolded, and you want to walk down to the bottom of the valley (the lowest Loss).

A normal derivative is for a 2D line. You only have one option: go left or right. But on a 3D mountain, you can walk North/South, or East/West.

A Partial Derivative just means: "Freeze one direction." If I lock my latitude (w₂) completely still so I can only step North or South (w₁), is the mountain sloping up or down in front of me?

2. Where does the massive equation come from?

To find the slope, we need an equation for the Total Loss. This is just middle school algebra (FOIL) on steroids.

Let's look only at House 1: Size = 1, Lot = 1.5, Actual Price = 5000.

  • Prediction: 1w₁ + 1.5w₂
  • Error: (1w₁ + 1.5w₂ - 5000)
  • Squared Error: (1w₁ + 1.5w₂ - 5000) × (1w₁ + 1.5w₂ - 5000)

If you multiply that out (FOIL), you get House 1's specific piece:
1(w₁)² + 3(w₁w₂) - 10000(w₁) + 2.25(w₂)² - 15000(w₂) + 25,000,000

If you do that exact same algebra for House 2, and then for House 3, and add all three together, you get our master Loss equation:

Loss = 3.125(w₁)² + 7.375(w₁)(w₂) - 26500(w₁) + 4.8125(w₂)² - 32750(w₂) + 57500000

3. Calculating the Slope

Let's put this monster equation into the Derivation Engine to find the Partial Derivative for w₁ (freezing w₂).

1. The Empty Term (The Boulder). Look at 4.8125(w₂)². Because we froze w₂, this whole chunk is just a static, unchanging number (like a boulder on the mountain). The slope of a static boulder is 0. It completely disappears! The same happens to -32750(w₂) and the 57500000.
Loss' = 3.125(w₁)² + 7.375(w₁)(w₂) - 26500(w₁) + 4.8125(w₂)² - 32750(w₂) + 57500000
2. The w₁ Only Term. Apply the basic power rule (bring the 2 down and multiply: 3.125 × 2 = 6.25).
→ 6.25(w₁) + 7.375(w₁)(w₂) - 26500(w₁)
3. The Tricky Mixed Term. Look at 7.375(w₁)(w₂). Since w₂ is frozen, 7.375(w₂) is just a constant number attached to w₁. So, the w₁ drops away, leaving just the multiplier behind. Same for the -26500(w₁).
→ 6.25(w₁) + 7.375(w₂) - 26500
4. Final Slope Equation. We now have the clean formulas to calculate the exact slopes! Try editing the weights in Module 1 to see these numbers recalculate.

Check Your Understanding: The Freezing Concept

Let's see if this "freezing" clicks. Here is a brand new, shorter dummy Loss equation:

Loss = 10(w₁)² + 5(w₁)(w₂) + 8(w₂)²

If we want to find the Partial Derivative for w₁ (meaning we freeze w₂ as a constant), what is the final slope equation for w₁?

Correct!

1. The 10(w₁)² becomes 20(w₁) (power rule).
2. In 5(w₁)(w₂), the 5(w₂) is just a constant multiplier, so the w₁ drops, leaving 5(w₂).
3. The 8(w₂)² has no w₁ in it at all, so it's a static "boulder" and disappears entirely to 0!
05

The Learning Step

We know the direction to move (decrease), but how big of an adjustment do we make? We don't want to overshoot the valley floor. We control this step size using a Learning Rate.

Type in a Learning Rate below (e.g., 0.01) to see exactly how the model updates its weights.

Update w₁
Old w₁ - (LR × Slope w₁)
1000 - (0.01 × 1875) = 981
Update w₂
Old w₂ - (LR × Slope w₂)
3000 - (0.01 × 3500) = 2965
